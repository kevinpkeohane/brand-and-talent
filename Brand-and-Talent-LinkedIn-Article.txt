Brand & Talent² — The book, and the four volumes that followed


I've been writing about brand, go-to-market strategy and employer brand for about twenty years. Not all of it was good. Some of it got better over time. A few pieces turned out to be genuinely useful — the kind where someone forwarded it to their CEO or came back six months later to say they'd used it on a real brief.

Brand & Talent² is what happens when you collect the useful ones, sequence them properly, and add the context that makes the difference between reading something and knowing what to do next.

It's 76 articles. Four parts: Decide, Build, Grow, Align. Free.

What I didn't expect was that some of the problems I'd written about were too big to live inside a single chapter. The kind of problem where someone needs 20 consecutive pieces on positioning — not four. Where one chapter on go-to-market isn't enough when the whole machine is broken. Where stalled growth needs 23 field notes on second-order causes, not a summary with three bullet points.

That's why there are now four standalone volumes. Each one takes a single growth problem and goes deep on it. No adjacent material. No filler to make it feel like a complete book.


The main guide

Brand & Talent² pulls together the full scope of work: how brands make decisions that hold, how go-to-market machinery gets built, how growth problems get diagnosed when the obvious answers don't work, and how you align people behind a strategy without it dissolving between floors.

The four parts follow a logic. You need to Decide before you can Build. If you've built it and you're still not growing, the answer is usually in Grow. And if the strategy is right but execution is fragmented, the problem is in Align.

Each article ends with a concrete next step. Not "consider whether this applies to your context." An actual next thing to do, based on the problem the article addresses.

The four volumes


2.1 — Decide

Most brands don't have a positioning problem. They have a decision problem.

The workshops happened. The deck exists. Someone paid an agency good money to produce something the leadership team reviewed and approved. And then nothing changed. Sales kept improvising. Marketing kept producing content that was fine but not distinctively theirs. The CEO was still answering "what makes you different?" with a version that didn't match anyone else's.

That's not a strategy failure. It's a decision that was never actually made.

Twenty field notes on the moment between knowing and deciding. How to find a position your competitors can't claim. What makes a narrative travel beyond the boardroom. When to reposition, and how to validate before you commit capital to a rebrand. What a rebrand actually needs to change to work.

For CEOs, CMOs and founders who know roughly what they should be — but haven't made the decision to actually be that thing.


2.2 — Build

The strategy is done. The positioning is right. The ICP is defined. The leadership team is aligned. The deck is good.

Then you hand it to the sales team and nothing happens. Six months later there are three different stories in market and none of them are winning deals.

The problem isn't the strategy. It's the machinery. Positioning without a GTM model is a belief system, not a commercial motion. You need messaging architecture that tells sales what to say and when. Demand generation that reflects how B2B buyers actually buy now. Enablement that reps use in the field rather than file on a shared drive. A model that connects all of it to pipeline metrics.

Twenty-three field notes on building the machinery between positioning and revenue. For revenue leaders who've done the strategy work and need the engine.


2.3 — Grow

Stalled growth usually isn't caused by one obvious thing. If it were, you'd have fixed it.

It's caused by a cluster of quiet misalignments. Between what the brand promises and what the organisation delivers. Between what AI is producing and what buyers actually find distinctive. Between the strategy you've approved and the accountability that was never attached to it.

Twenty-three field notes on growth problems that resist the standard diagnostics. Not the first-order issues — pricing, competition, bad product. The second-order ones that show up when everything looks like it should be working but the numbers say otherwise.

If your numbers are flat and your team can't tell you exactly why, start here.


2.4 — Align

This one's about a specific pattern I've seen more than any other.

Teams that are executing the same strategy but not the same version of it. Marketing with one story. Sales with another. The employer brand saying something that doesn't match either. Culture work that isn't connected to the customer promise. And underneath all of it, a gap between what leadership decided and what the people expected to deliver it actually understand.

Ten field notes on closing the gaps between sales, marketing, go-to-market, culture and the employee-to-customer experience. The shortest volume, because the problem — once you can name it — has a relatively clear path.


How to use them

Read the main guide if you want the full picture. It's structured so you can start anywhere and follow the problem.

Read the volumes if you have a specific problem and need to go deeper than a chapter allows.

The four volumes follow the same sequence as the guide: Decide before you Build; Grow before you can tell whether Align is the problem or the other way round. But they're standalone. If you know you have a go-to-market problem, start with 2.2. If growth has stalled and nothing obvious is broken, go to 2.3.
